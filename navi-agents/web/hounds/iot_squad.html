Argos, Odysseus’s dog, who knew his master after twenty years by the smallest signs — here he recognises the devices at your network edge from their fingerprints, and learns to recognise more.

The Problem

Cameras, controllers, printers, firewalls and lab gear sit at the network edge with no agent, no CMDB entry and no owner. Tenable often sees them in banners, certificates and service output — but nobody turns that evidence into an inventory.

What It Does

Argos is four cooperating agents. One confirms known devices from a detection registry; one finds the plugins on each confirmed host that carry the device's name; one sweeps those plugins across the estate and proposes new detections; one runs the loop and keeps a ledger of everything tested. You approve at two gates: tagging devices, and adding detections to the registry.

At a glance

Key Capabilities

  • Registry detections — known devices matched by plugin, plugin name or output — the registry grows as you approve new detections.
  • Signal fusion — device type, network-card vendor (OUI), local-network service announcements (mDNS) and certificates find devices the registry doesn't know yet.
  • Carrier-plugin harvest — host by host, the plugins whose name or output carries the device term.
  • Learning loop — a device term found in a carrier plugin on enough assets (at least 2, rising with the size of the estate) becomes a proposed detection — confirm, cross-reference or new.
  • Exposure on devices — critical, high and KEV findings and AES for each device.
  • Coverage you can trust — a QA ledger of every plugin-term combination tested, and which of the seven identity signals your scans collect.
  • Two human gates — Gate 1 tags devices Asset Inventory : <facet>: <device>; Gate 2 adds approved detections to the registry, exportable.

How It Works

Argos reads findings and plugin output in your local navi.db; nothing changes until you approve. If your scans are all credentialed agent or SSH scans of servers, the identity signals are absent and the squad finds little — the page says so instead of guessing. Tags go through navi behind the write gate and a confirm.

Who It's For

OT and IoT security teams who need an inventory they can defend, network teams hunting the unmanaged edge, and VM teams extending coverage beyond agents.

Works Better With

Argos's device tags share Linnaeus's Asset Inventory scheme, so classified devices can be owned (Ownership) and calibrated (Criticality Calibration); single candidate tags (IoT : <name>) feed Exposure Score's exposure factor. Automation Station re-applies the device tags on its schedule.
