The Problem

A CVE number says something is broken, not what an adversary does with it. Detection engineers think in ATT&CK techniques and tactics; vulnerability data arrives without that context, so two teams look at the same finding in two languages.

What It Does

MITRE ATT&CK Map fetches the public ATT&CK-to-CVE mapping from MITRE's Center for Threat-Informed Defense, matches it to the CVEs in your findings, and shows the techniques and tactics your unpatched estate enables on an ATT&CK matrix. He tags the affected assets with each CVE's primary impact, secondary impact and exploit technique.

At a glance

Key Capabilities

  • Public mapping, approved download — the Center for Threat-Informed Defense ATT&CK→CVE mapping, fetched after you approve it (or auto-approve in Settings), with a bundled snapshot offline.
  • Only what's in your estate — by default only CVEs present in navi.db, so every tag lands on real assets.
  • ATT&CK matrix — techniques and tactics your findings enable, by mapping type; full screen and email from the widget.
  • Mitre tags — Mitre : Primary Impact: <technique>, Secondary Impact: <technique> and Exploit Technique: <technique> on the assets carrying each CVE, via navi's tag-by-CVE.
  • Honest coverage — not every CVE has a mapped technique; an unmapped CVE simply gets no Mitre tag.
  • By tag — run over one tag's assets (an owner, a tier, an environment): the ATT&CK exposure, matrix, planned tags and ACR > 7 list cover only that tag, and tags written from that view land only on its assets. Switch between the whole estate and your last 5 tags without re-running.

How It Works

MITRE ATT&CK Map reads CVEs from your local navi.db and joins the public mapping. Tags use navi enrich tag --cve — the CVE picks the assets — with the category fixed to Mitre, behind the write gate and a confirm.

Who It's For

Threat-informed defense programs mapping exposure to techniques, and detection engineers who want to know which techniques their unpatched estate enables.

Works Better With

Laelaps says a vulnerability is exploited; MITRE ATT&CK Map says how it's used. Fenrir labels each attack-path step with its ATT&CK technique, Exposure Score counts ATT&CK techniques in its score, and Automation Station re-applies the Mitre tags on its schedule.

Why It's Different

  • Context on your findings — not another threat feed to read — labels on the vulnerabilities you already track.
  • Bridges two teams — vulnerability management and detection engineering sort the same list the same way.
  • Nothing invented — every technique comes from the public mapping.
